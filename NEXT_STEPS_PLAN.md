## Question 2: What will be the nature of the solution?

### Our Approach: **Hybrid Solution** (Technical + Behavioral + Social)

We believe the solution needs multiple components:

### 1. **Technical Component** (Primary)
**What:** A physical device/system that removes lint from the air

**Options we're exploring:**
- Air filtration systems (fans + filters)
- Ventilation improvements
- Source capture (capturing lint at the loom)
- Combination approaches

**Why technical is needed:**
- The problem is physical (airborne lint)
- Requires a physical intervention
- Can be measured objectively

**Constraints:**
- Must be affordable (under ₹10,000)
- Must use minimal power
- Must be simple to install and maintain
- Must work in existing workshop spaces

### 2. **Behavioral Component** (Supporting)
**What:** Changes in how workshops operate

**Examples:**
- Regular cleaning schedules
- Proper maintenance routines
- Usage protocols for the technical solution

**Why behavioral is needed:**
- Technical solutions only work if used correctly
- Maintenance is critical for long-term success
- Workers need to adopt new habits

**How we'll address it:**
- Simple, visual instructions
- Training sessions
- Easy-to-follow maintenance schedules
- Design for "set and forget" where possible

### 3. **Social Component** (Enabling)
**What:** Community support and adoption

**Examples:**
- Peer learning (workshop owners teaching each other)
- Group purchasing for better prices
- Sharing success stories
- Building trust in the solution

**Why social is needed:**
- Small workshops learn from each other
- Group adoption creates momentum
- Community validation increases trust
- Can reduce costs through bulk purchasing

**How we'll address it:**
- Start with a few pilot workshops
- Document success stories
- Create simple sharing mechanisms
- Build a network of early adopters

### 4. **Economic Component** (Critical)
**What:** Making it financially viable

**Considerations:**
- Affordable initial cost
- Low operating costs
- Clear ROI for workshop owners
- Potential financing options

**Why economic is critical:**
- Small workshops operate on tight margins
- Must demonstrate value quickly
- May need payment plans or subsidies

---

### Most Likely Solution Type: **Technical (with behavioral support)**

**Primary:** A low-cost air filtration or ventilation system
**Supporting:** Simple maintenance protocols and usage guidelines
**Enabling:** Community adoption and peer learning

**Why this combination:**
- Technical solution addresses the root cause (airborne lint)
- Behavioral component ensures it's used effectively
- Social component enables adoption at scale

## Question 3: How are we going to collect pictorial evidence?

### Evidence Collection Strategy

### Phase 1: Permission & Planning (Before Visits)

**Step 1: Get Consent**
- Contact workshop owners in advance
- Explain the project purpose
- Ask permission to photograph
- Offer to share photos with them
- Respect privacy requests (blur faces if needed)

**Step 2: Prepare Equipment**
- Smartphone cameras (most accessible)
- Optional: DSLR for better quality
- Measuring tape for scale
- Notebook for notes

**Step 3: Plan What to Capture**
- Wide shots of entire workshop
- Close-ups of lint accumulation
- Machine details showing lint buildup
- Ceiling and walls showing dust
- Workers (with permission)
- Ventilation points (or lack thereof)
- Any existing solutions they've tried

### Phase 2: During Visits

**Photo Checklist:**

**Workshop Overview:**
- [ ] Exterior of workshop
- [ ] Interior wide shot showing all looms
- [ ] Layout and spacing

**Lint Evidence:**
- [ ] Close-up of lint on machines (gears, frames)
- [ ] Lint on ceiling and walls
- [ ] Lint on floor
- [ ] Lint on fabric being woven
- [ ] Airborne lint (if visible in light beam)

**Infrastructure:**
- [ ] Ventilation (windows, doors, fans)
- [ ] Electrical setup
- [ ] Room dimensions (use person/object for scale)
- [ ] Ceiling height

**People & Process:**
- [ ] Workers (with permission, faces blurred if requested)
- [ ] Loom operation
- [ ] Cleaning activities
- [ ] Any protective equipment used

**Existing Solutions:**
- [ ] Any filters or devices already installed
- [ ] Cleaning tools and methods
- [ ] What they've tried before

### Phase 3: Organization & Storage

**File Naming Convention:**
```
YYYYMMDD_Location_Subject_###.jpg

Examples:
20251203_Workshop_A_LintOnMachine_001.jpg
20251203_Workshop_A_WideShot_002.jpg
20251203_Workshop_B_CeilingLint_001.jpg
```

**Storage:**
- Upload to GitHub: `08_Assets/Photos/Workshop_Photos/`
- Also backup to Google Drive or similar
- Create a simple spreadsheet listing all photos with descriptions

**Privacy:**
- Get written/verbal consent before photographing people
- Blur faces if requested
- Don't share identifiable photos publicly without permission
- Keep contact information private

### Phase 4: Documentation

**For Each Photo:**
- Date and location
- What it shows
- Why it's important
- Any relevant measurements or notes

**Create Photo Log:**
- Simple spreadsheet or document
- Links photos to observations
- Makes it easy to find specific evidence later

---

### Practical Tips for Evidence Collection

**1. Start with Phone Cameras**
- Everyone has one
- Good enough quality
- Easy to share immediately
- Can upgrade later if needed

**2. Take More Than You Think You Need**
- Better to have too many than too few
- Can delete later
- Different angles tell different stories

**3. Include Scale References**
- Person standing next to machine
- Common object (water bottle, notebook)
- Measuring tape visible in shot

**4. Document Context**
- What was happening when photo was taken?
- Time of day
- How many looms were running
- Any unusual conditions

**5. Respect Privacy**
- Always ask before photographing people
- Explain how photos will be used
- Offer to share copies with workshop owners
- Blur faces if requested

**6. Show Before/After (Later)**
- When testing solutions, take photos from same angles
- Makes impact visible and compelling
